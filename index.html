Access Control & Theft

§2.3–2.6
Step 1 — Does the property have traceable access control?
Traceable access control means the system links each market entry to a specific unit, resident, or named individual — such as individual FOB keys, key cards, mobile credentials, or biometric access. A shared door code or building-wide PIN does not qualify.
Yes — traceable access control
Property uses individual FOBs, key cards, mobile credentials, or biometrics linked to a specific unit or person. Theft can be traced. All three theft protocol options are available.
No — no traceable access control
Entry is not individually traceable. Client is financially liable for all theft at vendor's wholesale cost. Vendor provides camera access link and inventory reports. Client management still must communicate theft notices.
Always included in every contract regardless of current access status: If the property loses, removes, or downgrades its access control system at any point during the term such that individual traceability is no longer possible, the Client's financial liability for theft automatically shifts to the no-traceable-access structure from that date forward. Client must notify Vendor in writing within five (5) business days. Failure to notify is a material breach.
No traceable access — theft & liability
✓ Fixed language for this path Because the Property does not have an access control system capable of linking market entry to a specific unit or responsible individual, the Client assumes full financial responsibility for all theft at the Market Location. The Vendor shall provide the Client with a permanent shared access link to the Market's camera system — the Client may create its own account and view live and recorded footage at any time at no cost. The Vendor makes no warranty as to footage retention duration, as storage depends on activity volume. The Vendor shall provide quarterly inventory reconciliation reports. Any unexplained discrepancy constitutes the Theft Loss at the Vendor's wholesale cost. The Vendor bears no obligation to identify perpetrators or generate incident reports. The Client's management team shall forward the Vendor's theft notices and include written warnings of potential reporting to local authorities, non-renewal of lease, and eviction (as permitted under Florida law) within five (5) business days of receipt. The Client is not required to follow through. The Theft Loss shall be deducted from any revenue share or fee owed by the Vendor to the Client for that quarter. If the Theft Loss exceeds the amount owed, the Client shall pay the remaining balance to the Vendor within fifteen (15) days of the quarterly report.
Step 2 — Theft protocol
Since the property has traceable access control, you can choose how theft is handled. All options include a permanent camera access link for the client and quarterly inventory reconciliation reports.
Option A — Good faith only
Vendor absorbs all theft. Client management must forward reports and send written threats (authorities, non-renewal, eviction) within 5 business days. No financial liability on client. Failure to communicate is a breach subject to the cure period.
Option B — Full fine protocol
Client management forwards reports AND imposes fines through the building's lease system. Fines offset theft loss. Surplus split 50/50. Good-faith failure capped at revenue share. Non-compliance = full wholesale cost.
Option C — Vendor-led enforcement
Vendor imposes all fines directly. Client applies lease pressure (warnings, non-renewal, eviction) within 5 business days. Vendor may report to authorities. Good-faith failure capped at revenue share. Non-compliance = full wholesale cost.
Option A — Good faith communication. No financial liability on client ever. The Vendor absorbs all Theft Losses. The Client's management team shall forward the Vendor's theft reports to the identified resident within five (5) business days of receipt and shall include written warnings of potential reporting to local law enforcement, non-renewal of lease, and eviction proceedings as permitted under applicable Florida law. The Client is not required to follow through on such actions. The Client shall confirm in writing to the Vendor what communications were sent and on what date within the same five (5) business day window. Failure to forward communications and confirm delivery within the required period shall constitute a breach of this Agreement subject to the cure provisions of Section 3.2.
